The business question

After reducing the workforce, can the business still deliver its services reliably and sustainably, and where are the risks?

What I found

−25%

The cut fell on community access

Community access lost about 172 hours a month, more than the whole business lost. Overnight growth at one site (+83%) hid most of it.

0% → 43%

The business now does different work

Onsite and overnight support grew from nothing to 43% of hours in a year. Shifts got longer overall because of this mix, not because individual services changed.

32% → 45%

A concentration risk

Almost half of all hours now come from one site and a small group of workers. Two suburbs worth about 11% of hours are no longer served.

−73%

Hard-to-staff slots disappeared

Evening work almost vanished and weekends fell 18%. The data can't say whether demand dropped or staff weren't available, so I made that the first question to answer.

What I recommended

Each recommendation came with the reason behind it and a concrete first step the team could take the next week.

  1. Find out why community, evening and weekend hours fellIf staff shortages caused it, the business is turning away work it could deliver. First step: review ShiftCare's vacant and declined shift reports for April to July.
  2. Reduce dependence on one site45% of hours rely on one site and a few workers. First step: train a backup pool for onsite and overnight shifts through buddy shifts.
  3. Recruit for hard-to-fill slotsEvenings (−73%) and weekends (−18%) fell most. First step: target job ads at workers available for those times.
  4. Monitor the workforce monthlyThe size and timing of the reduction weren't recorded anywhere. First step: refresh the dashboard monthly, adding headcount, vacant and cancelled shifts.
  5. Improve data quality3% of shifts had no location and exports overlapped. First step: make the address field mandatory and export full calendar months.

How I prepared the data

Step 1Combine

Merged 6 ShiftCare exports in Power Query and removed shifts duplicated where date ranges overlapped.

Step 2Standardise

Cleaned suburb names and flagged missing locations instead of dropping them.

Step 3Group

Mapped 18 shift types into 7 service groups the business recognises.

Step 4Compare fairly

Used monthly and daily averages, since the before and after periods differ in length.

Shared with Darling Daughters' permission. Based on de-identified scheduling data: no client names, worker names or pay information were used.
