The process I redesigned

I mapped how a buyer's quote request moved through the business, found where time was lost, then redesigned it around an AI agent with a person still making the call.

Two designs, tested side by side

I built a simple AI workflow first as a baseline, then an AI agent that can look things up before it answers.

    Same request, very different answers

    Both workflows received the same test request. The difference shows why grounding AI in company data matters.

    It never checked capacity or certification data. It just sounded sure.

    Workflow 2: grounded and careful
    Internal summary (excerpt)

    Feasibility: Possible fit. Target price within typical shirt price band, but capacity, OEKO-TEX evidence and lead time need verification.

    Decision score: 72/100
    Key risks: OEKO-TEX documentation; 6-week timeline vs capacity

    Next action: request fabric spec and size breakdown; ask production to check rush capacity.

    The price check came from the pricing table (US$4–11.50 for shirts), not from the model's guess.

    Consistency check: across three runs the agent gave the same verdict ("possible fit") and the same two risks every time.

    The agent's draft, ready for review

    Saved as a Gmail draft. Nothing is sent until a person approves it.

    Preliminary response to RFQ – 60,000 Shirt/Blouse – Bright Schoolwear

    Dear Bright Schoolwear Team,

    Thank you for your enquiry… Based on our preliminary assessment, this request appears potentially feasible. Cotton poplin shirts are a common product category and your target price sits within typical industry price bands for shirts, subject to final fabric quality, trims, workmanship and certification costs.

    Final pricing, production capacity, certification evidence, and delivery commitment must be reviewed and confirmed by our sales and production teams.

    To prepare an accurate formal quotation, please provide:

    • Fabric specification (GSM, composition, weave)
    • Tech pack, size breakdown and grading rules
    • Colourways and Pantone references
    • Trims, packaging, Incoterm and destination port

    Kind regards,
    HaBac Garco Sales Team

    Built to be safe in a real business

    Human approval

    Emails are saved as drafts. The sales team reviews pricing, timing and claims before anything is sent.

    No invented facts

    The agent is instructed never to invent prices, capacity, lead times or certifications, and to flag what needs checking.

    Internal vs external

    The fit score and risks stay in the internal summary. The buyer only sees a careful, preliminary reply.

    Audit trail

    Every request, summary, score and draft is logged in Google Sheets for follow-up and review.

    What I'd fix next

    • The capability lookup returned no match in testing, so the agent's feasibility view was only partly evidence-based. The fix: make the form's product options match the sheet's category labels exactly, then re-run the test.
    • The fit score looks more precise than it is. Three runs on the same request gave 66, 66 and 72. The verdict and risks stayed the same, but the score moved 6 points, so its weighting should be fixed and written down before staff rely on it.
    • It's only as good as its data. Pricing and capability sheets need an owner and regular updates before real use.

    How I'd prove it before going live

    The prototype was tested on a small number of requests. Before the sales team relies on it, this is the evaluation I would run.

    1. A test set of 15 requests

    Three of each type, each with the answer a salesperson would give.

    ScenarioThe agent should
    Clear fitSay "strong fit" and ask only for missing specs
    Price too lowFlag the price politely, without rejecting the buyer
    Impossible deadlineFlag the timeline for a rush-order check
    Missing detailsList exactly what is needed to quote
    Product not madeSay it is a low fit, without inventing capability

    2. Pass criteria, then a pilot

    Clear targets, agreed with the sales team in advance.

    • 12 of 15 verdicts match what a salesperson would decide.
    • Zero buyer emails that promise price, capacity, timing or certification.
    • Every incomplete request has its missing details flagged.
    • Pilot: run alongside the sales team on 20 real past requests, comparing replies and timing the real time saved.
