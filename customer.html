Case 1 · Diagnose

FreshMart: a store with loyal big spenders, and too few of them

A grocery store with falling sales, compared with its two main rivals using a survey of over 300 shoppers. The question: is the problem attracting customers, keeping their spend, or how often they come?

Breaking market share into its parts

Market share = how many shoppers use the store × share of their grocery spend it gets × how heavily they buy. FreshMart vs its two competitors.

The diagnosis: FreshMart doesn't have a spending problem. It has a reach and loyalty problem. Too few shoppers use it, and those who do take most of their spend elsewhere.

Net Promoter Score: −60

And assortment is exactly where FreshMart rates worst: 1.4 out of 5, against roughly 2.7 to 2.9 for rivals.

What I recommended

  • Grow reach before anything else. Penetration is the biggest gap, and brands with fewer customers also get weaker loyalty (the double jeopardy pattern). Use partnerships, campaigns and value bundles to win new households.
  • Fix assortment first. It is the strongest driver of satisfaction and FreshMart's weakest rating: audit stock-outs, widen and deepen the range, and add products rivals don't carry.
  • Lean into the high spenders. Its above-average spend per customer supports a curated, premium position, protected by a loyalty program with personalised rewards.
Case 2 · Design

Homer & Co: customers will pay for speed

A roofing manufacturer facing cheaper foreign competitors wanted to know which product to build. Conjoint analysis measured how customers trade off four features against each other.

The winning product

Of nine possible product bundles, one dominated.

Bundle 1: 1-week install, flawless finish, premium $10 price89%predicted purchase probability, despite the highest price

The model correctly predicted 17 of 20 respondents' top choice (85% accuracy). With only 20 respondents, results are directional and should be validated with a larger sample.

Three customer segments

Different buyers, different priorities. Bundle 1 led in all three.

45% of buyersSpeed-oriented

Install speed is 50% of their decision. Time-pressed homeowners and urgent repairs.

40% of buyersDurability-focused

Lifespan is 49% of their decision. Roofing as a long-term investment.

15% of buyersValue-balanced

An even mix of factors. A niche for a mid-tier, value-for-money option.

Recommendation: protect the one-week installation advantage (competitors need 1.2 to 1.5 weeks) with process investment and a "1-week installation guarantee", and launch Bundle 1 at the premium price.

Case 3 · Defend

GiftMate: losing the lead to a privacy-first rival

A personalised-gifting market leader faces SnapGift, a new competitor built on data privacy. Using 300 customers' ratings and priorities, a choice model predicted what happens to market share, and what GiftMate can do about it.

Try it: can GiftMate win its share back?

This runs the actual choice model on all 300 survey respondents. Improve how customers rate GiftMate and see the predicted market share after SnapGift's entry.

Today: 7.2 out of 10. SnapGift: 9.0.
Today: 6.1 out of 10, the lowest of all four brands.

Black marks show predicted shares with no response from GiftMate. Before SnapGift arrived, GiftMate held 44.6%.

What the model reveals: a 2-point privacy gain alone still leaves GiftMate in second place. A smaller privacy gain (+1) combined with better price perception (+1.5) puts it back in first, because customers weigh all four attributes almost equally.

Each customer's attraction to a brand = their importance weights × their ratings of that brand. A multinomial logit model turns attraction into purchase probability, averaged across all 300 customers. Ratings are capped at 10.

Customers weigh price, personalisation, variety and privacy almost equally (about 25% each), so privacy had become a core reason to choose, and the market leader had the most to lose.

Did the ad campaigns work?

Brand metrics before and after two campaigns.

Net reach–25%43%

Campaign 2 kept building awareness, but purchase intention fell back. And privacy ratings rose only from 7.0 to 7.4, too small to close the gap (try "After its ad campaigns" in the simulator).

What I recommended

  • Make privacy a product change, not a message. Awareness is already high. Ads alone moved privacy ratings by less than half a point, so GiftMate needs visible privacy features and transparent data practices.
  • Keep personalisation as the lead strength. GiftMate rates highest of all brands on it (8.9 out of 10). Position it as "personalised and private" rather than trading one for the other.
  • Shift the next campaign from reach to conversion. Recall is at 88%, but purchase intention dropped after campaign 2.
